Handscroll

2026HTML · CSS · JS6 min read

A personal website you unroll instead of open. One wax seal, two paper rolls, and a long sheet that keeps going as you scroll.

01The idea

Most personal sites start with information: a name, a sentence, a few links. This one starts with an object. People want to touch an object; they only skim a paragraph.

So the first thing you do here is break a seal. It is a single click, but the scroll answers with two seconds of motion: the wax lifts and falls, the twine slides off, the rolls part and the paper settles flat.

02One feature, one folder

The first version kept everything in one page: the wax SVG, five filters, the unroll logic and the language switch. It worked, and it was impossible to read.

Now every piece lives in its own folder under features/, with its own styles, script, demo page and notes. The pieces never import each other. They talk through a handful of events on the document, like seal:broken and scroll:opened.

  • Delete a folder and the rest of the site still runs.
  • Open any demo.html to see one piece alone.
  • Colours, timing and easing come only from core/tokens.css.

03Reading right to left

A real handscroll is read from right to left: you unroll with one hand and roll up with the other. The homepage does the same. Scrolling down feeds the paper out from the left roller, and the wooden grain turns as it goes.

Motion should have a reason. If nothing physical would move, nothing on screen moves.

04What I would do next

Sound, maybe: paper and cracking wax, off by default. A real curl rendered in canvas. And more leaves in the scroll, which is the only part that really matters.
